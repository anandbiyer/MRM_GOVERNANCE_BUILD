### G0.22 / FT-04 — is the ordering assertion a sound proof?

**Yes for the defect it was written for, and the hand-over's doubt is warranted for one narrower
class.** Two mutations:

| # | Mutation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| A | Restore the original defect: move the status restore to *after* `start_analysis_job` | **Fails** `test_ft_04_the_reset_writes_nothing_after_starting_the_evidence_analysis`, and nothing else in `test_imports.py` or `test_documents.py` |
| B | The same post-submit write, but issued on the **Engine** (`engine.begin()` + `exec_driver_sql`) instead of a Session | *Survives* — 28/28 import tests pass → **TR-34 (Low, new)** |

Mutation A is the decisive result: the ordering test is the *only* test in the suite that catches the
regression that cost all 1,116 evidence documents, and it catches it alone. The decision to assert
ordering rather than the race is correct and the docstring says exactly why.

What it proves is slightly narrower than it reads, though. It proves *"no `Session` commit after the
submit"*, which is a proxy for *"no write after the submit"*. Two post-submit writes race the
background job identically and are invisible to it: a write issued on the Engine or a raw DBAPI
connection (mutation B, confirmed), and an **uncommitted** write transaction still open at submit
time, which holds SQLite's write lock just as effectively while firing no `after_commit` at all. The
project's layering invariant means all realistic writes go through a Session, so this is a Low
residual rather than a hole — but the docstring should say so, or the listener should move to the
`Engine`-level `"commit"` event.
